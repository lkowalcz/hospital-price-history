A NOTE ON INTERPRETATION

A change in the file
deserves a closer look.

Use this page to find changes worth investigating. Open the original files to understand their context.

What counts as a match?

Entries match only when their code type, code, and description are identical. Leading zeros are preserved. A renamed description appears as one removed entry and one added entry; it is not automatically treated as a price change.

What do these prices mean?

Cash prices and gross charges are the median of distinct positive values within each code-and-description group under method 2. Negotiated minimums and maximums span dollar-denominated payer rates. These summaries combine payer and service-setting details; a change can reflect a different mix of entries. They are not insurer-specific or patient-specific estimates.

Percent changes are calculated only when both values are positive. Blank, zero, negative, and non-finite values do not establish a comparable positive price. Missing values are never treated as zero.

Why do some comparisons need review?

Snapshots produced with different summarization methods cannot be compared here. Source warnings are displayed above the results. Changes larger than fourfold in either direction are marked for review; they remain visible and are not automatically interpreted as price increases or decreases for equivalent care.

What do the dates and downloads represent?

A snapshot date is the date the summary entered this archive. The source file may have been observed earlier, particularly when a summary was rebuilt. Price changes could have happened between observations; these dates do not establish an effective date.

CSV downloads include every filtered entry, not just the visible page. They preserve source price strings; displayed amounts are rounded to at most six decimal places. Text that could be interpreted as a spreadsheet formula is prefixed with an apostrophe.
